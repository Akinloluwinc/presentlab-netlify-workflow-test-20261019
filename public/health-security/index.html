The practice

From policy to operational readiness.

Present Lab works across preparedness, biosecurity, policy and surveillance. Our experience in Nigeria’s port-health system connects institutional commitments with the people, procedures and resources needed to act.

We work across hazards and use a One Health approach where human, animal and environmental health intersect. Each engagement starts with the facility, corridor or institution: its responsibilities, constraints and relationships.

  • Ministries of health and IHR National Focal Points
  • Port, airport and ground-crossing authorities
  • Public health and security agencies
  • Regional health bodies and delivery partners
  • Funders and private operators

What we do

Four lines
of work.

Commission a defined assignment or connect the work into a longer programme of institutional readiness.

01 / Health security

Points of entry readiness

Assess capacities, develop contingency plans, rehearse decisions and train teams at airports, seaports and ground crossings. Our approach draws on the International Health Regulations and WHO assessment guidance, adapted to the setting.

02 / Biosecurity

Connecting public health and security

Clarify responsibilities, reporting pathways and access to resources during biological threats. We support biological-risk governance and exercises that examine how public health and security institutions work together.

03 / Policy and institutions

Making commitments workable

Translate policy and technical requirements into procedures, accountability and practical implementation priorities. Support cross-border information sharing and coordination, with clear institutional ownership.

04 / Surveillance

Designing useful early warning

Assess the feasibility of emerging approaches, including aircraft wastewater surveillance, and connect surveillance at points of entry with national systems. Examine costs, responsibilities and how information will inform decisions.

How an engagement runs

Score. Build. Test. Return.

A baseline gives the programme its direction. Six stages connect assessment with practical improvements and a repeat review using consistent methods.

Measure what the evidence can support.Scores describe what was evidenced at the time of assessment. They help prioritise action; they do not certify readiness. Reassessment can track changes in assessed capacities, while sustained performance needs evidence from practice.
  1. 01

    Score

    Assess the facility against agreed criteria using interviews, document review and observation. Identify evidenced capacities, gaps and priorities.

    Output: A baseline assessment with transparent scoring and limitations.

  2. 02

    Plan

    Develop a contingency plan that works across hazards, with procedures adapted to the facility and its responsibilities.

    Output: A practical plan, response protocols and agreed actions.

  3. 03

    Rehearse

    Use tabletop and simulation exercises to test decisions, coordination and procedures. Debrief with participants and turn findings into actions.

    Output: Exercise materials, an evaluation and a gap-closure plan.

  4. 04

    Train

    Build training around the gaps identified. Use practical scenarios and knowledge checks to support the people expected to implement the plan.

    Output: Reusable materials, facilitated sessions and a learning review.

  5. 05

    Integrate

    Connect facility procedures with port-wide, state and national arrangements. Agree responsibilities, reporting routes and follow-up ownership.

    Output: Documented coordination arrangements and an implementation plan.

  6. 06

    Re-score

    Repeat the assessment using consistent criteria. Examine what has changed, what remains unresolved and where to focus next.

    Output: A comparable reassessment and updated priorities.

Capacity transfer

Capability that stays with the team.

Training connects the plan to the people who will use it. Sessions leave teams with practical tools, reusable materials and a clearer basis for follow-through.
